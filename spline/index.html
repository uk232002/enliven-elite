Fig. 01 — Plan

Why Floor Plans are Essential

A plan is the argument before the building. It resolves circulation, daylight and proportion while changes still cost pencil rather than concrete — and it gives every trade one shared source of truth.

Fig. 02 — Interior

Why 3D Interior Designing Matters

Clients do not read plans, they read rooms. Modelling the interior turns dimension lines into material, light and volume — so decisions about finish and furniture happen before anything is ordered.

Fig. 03 — Shell

The Moment A Plan Stands Up

Slabs, walls and stairs first, and nothing else. Massing exposes scale, overshadowing and roofline against context — the problems flat drawings hide until they are expensive to fix.

Fig. 04 — Rooms

Furnished Before A Wall Is Skinned

Layout is argued while it is still cheap to move. Seating, circulation and storage go in against the real structure, so the plan is tested at the size people will actually live at.

Fig. 05 — Glazing

Where The Light Gets In

Openings are the last structural argument and the first spatial one. Set the glass and the rooms behind it stop being floor area and start being morning, afternoon and privacy.

Cladding, screens and balconies go on last, over a house that already works. The elevation is the consequence of the plan — not a drawing the plan was bent to fit.
